Part 4 - Understanding the Limitations
1. Which of these sentences were classified incorrectly?

    The sentence “the movie was not good” was classified incorrectly. The sentence has a negative meaning, 
    but the model predicted it as positive. This happened because the model focused on the word “good”, which 
    usually appears in positive reviews, and did not properly understand the negation created by the word “not”.

2. Why might the model struggle with sentences that contain negations like "not good" or "not bad"?

    The model struggles with negations because the bag-of-words approach treats words independently 
    and ignores their relationships. It does not understand that the word “not” changes the meaning of 
    words like “good” or “bad”. As a result, the model may classify “not good” as positive because it 
    only recognizes the word “good”.

3.  Why might sarcasm or mixed opinions be difficult for this type of model?

    Sarcasm or mixed opinions contain both positive and negative words in the same sentence. 
    Because the bag-of-words model only counts words and does not understand context or tone,
    it cannot determine the real intention of the sentence. This can lead to incorrect predictions 
    when the sentiment is subtle or contradictory.

4️. What information about the sentence meaning is lost when we only count words?

    When we only count words, important information such as word order, context, and relationships 
    between words is lost. The model cannot understand how words interact within a sentence or capture 
    the full meaning of the text. This makes it difficult for the model to correctly interpret negation, 
    sarcasm, or complex expressions.

Part 5 – From Bag-of-Words to Modern NLP
1️.  What is the main limitation of representing text using only word counts?

    The main limitation of representing text using only word counts is that the model does not understand 
    the meaning or context of words. The bag-of-words approach only counts how often words appear in a sentence 
    and ignores word order and relationships between words. Because of this, the model cannot fully understand 
    the meaning of the text.

2️.  Why might a model that understands word context perform better?

    A model that understands word context can perform better because it considers how words relate to each other 
    in a sentence. Modern NLP models such as word embeddings or transformer models can capture the meaning of words b
    ased on their context. This allows the model to better understand phrases, negations, and the overall meaning of 
    a sentence.

3️. What kinds of problems in language might require more advanced NLP models?

    More advanced NLP models are needed for language problems that require deeper understanding of meaning and 
    context. Examples include sarcasm detection, understanding complex sentences, question answering, machine 
    translation, and conversations in chatbots. These tasks require models that can understand relationships b
    etween words and the overall structure of language.
